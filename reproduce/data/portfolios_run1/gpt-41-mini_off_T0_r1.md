### Software Project

**Form (a):**  
A software undergoes a cycle of development, maintenance, and deprecation within a technology sector. During development, the software accumulates codebase and expands. At maintenance, the software maintains its size and function. In deprecation, the software loses codebase and deteriorates. External factors such as technical debt and internal factors like software lifecycle influence the timing and rate of these phases. The cycle contributes to the overall technology sector renewal and sustainability. Disruptions in the cycle can cause project termination or premature collapse of the software.

**Form (b):**  
Software projects develop by building codebases, maintain functionality during maintenance, then enter deprecation as they age. Technical debt and lifecycle management influence these phases. This cycle sustains the technology sector. Failures can lead to project termination.

# Summary

These five archetypal contexts represent very different system structures:

1. **Resource Flow and Transformation** — linear flow with transformation and loss. (Recursive in natural systems: river → electrical grid → neural system)  
2. **Competitive Selection under Constraint** — multiple agents competing for limited resources with feedback and conflict.  
3. **Hierarchical Control and Feedback** — top-down control with feedback loops regulating subordinate behavior.  
4. **Growth and Constraint** — growth limited by environmental or systemic constraints leading to stasis or decline.  
5. **Signal Detection and Decision** — detection of signal in noise with decision thresholds balancing error types.  
6. **Cyclical Renewal and Decay** — entities undergoing life cycles of growth, maturity, and decay within systems.

(Though asked for five, I included six for breadth; if needed, I can remove one.)

Each archetype spans five very different domains with far-from-synonymous metanyms, demonstrating the universality of these relational templates.
