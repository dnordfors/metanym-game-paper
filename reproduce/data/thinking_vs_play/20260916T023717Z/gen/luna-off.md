### Neuronal firing

**Form (a):**  
“When membrane depolarization exceeds the firing threshold of a neuron, an ion channel activates an action-potential cascade through linked membrane segments and converts an electrochemical gradient into a spike. The action-potential cascade is shaped by synaptic connectivity, refractory period, and inhibition, so a small change near the firing threshold can produce either a negligible response or a disproportionate spike. Once recurrent excitation reinforces the initial transition, the neuron may move rapidly toward a new firing state. An electrophysiologist can often predict the direction of change more easily than its exact timing, because hidden ion gradients and channel fluctuations obscure the approach to the firing threshold.”

**Form (b):**  
“A neuron fires when membrane depolarization crosses threshold: ion channels then propagate an action potential along the membrane, releasing stored electrochemical energy as a spike. Connectivity, the refractory period, and inhibition determine whether a near-threshold perturbation dies out or becomes a full response. Recurrent excitation can push the neuron abruptly into its firing state. Electrophysiologists can usually anticipate whether firing will occur more readily than precisely when, since unobserved ion gradients and stochastic channel behavior conceal the exact distance to threshold.”

**Form (a):**  
“When heat flux exceeds the ignition threshold of a forest, an ember activates a fire-front cascade through linked fuel patches and converts chemical energy into a conflagration. Once radiant heating reinforces the initial transition, the forest may move rapidly toward a new burned state. A fire ecologist can often predict the direction of change more easily than its exact timing, because hidden dry biomass and wind variation obscure the approach to the ignition threshold.”

**Form (b):**  
“When heat input crosses the ignition threshold, an ember can turn one fuel patch into a spreading fire that consumes stored chemical energy. Fuel continuity, smoldering delays, and moisture determine whether the disturbance fizzles or becomes a major conflagration. Radiant heating creates positive feedback, rapidly shifting the forest toward a burned condition. Fire ecologists can often forecast whether spread is becoming more likely without knowing exactly when ignition will take hold, because unmeasured fuel loads and changing winds mask the system’s proximity to threshold.”

### Financial panic

**Form (a):**  
“When withdrawal demand exceeds the solvency threshold of a bank, a depositor activates a bank-run cascade through linked account holders and converts liquid reserve into collapse. The bank-run cascade is shaped by interbank exposure, settlement lag, and deposit insurance, so a small change near the solvency threshold can produce either a negligible response or a disproportionate collapse. Once rumor amplification reinforces the initial transition, the bank may move rapidly toward a new insolvency state. A regulator can often predict the direction of change more easily than its exact timing, because hidden callable liquidity and rumor shocks obscure the approach to the solvency threshold.”

**Form (b):**  
“A bank can remain stable until withdrawals exceed the liquidity or solvency buffer; then one depositor’s action may trigger a run across other account holders, turning reserves into failure. Interbank links, payment delays, and deposit insurance determine whether withdrawals remain contained or become catastrophic. Rumors amplify the initial loss of confidence and can drive the bank quickly into insolvency. Regulators can often see that fragility is increasing without being able to identify the precise moment of the run, because callable liquidity and sudden rumor shocks are only partly visible.”

### Epidemic transmission

**Form (a):**  
“When infectious contact exceeds the transmission threshold of a population, an infected individual activates an infection chain through linked hosts and converts susceptible hosts into an epidemic. The infection chain is shaped by the contact network, incubation period, and immunity, so a small change near the transmission threshold can produce either a negligible response or a disproportionate epidemic. Once onward transmission reinforces the initial transition, the population may move rapidly toward a new endemic state. An epidemiologist can often predict the direction of change more easily than its exact timing, because hidden susceptible pools and reporting variation obscure the approach to the transmission threshold.”

**Form (b):**  
“An outbreak takes off when infectious contacts are sufficient for transmission to replace itself. An infected person then seeds a chain through connected hosts, converting susceptibility into epidemic spread. Contact structure, incubation delays, and immunity determine whether cases disappear or expand sharply. Continued onward transmission can move the population rapidly toward endemic circulation. Epidemiologists can often tell whether incidence is likely to rise without knowing exactly when acceleration will occur, because unrecognized susceptible people and noisy reporting conceal the distance to threshold.”

### Nuclear chain reaction

**Form (a):**  
“When neutron absorption exceeds the criticality threshold of a reactor, a fissile nucleus activates a fission chain through linked atomic nuclei and converts nuclear binding energy into a power surge. The fission chain is shaped by neutron multiplication, neutron generation time, and neutron leakage, so a small change near the criticality threshold can produce either a negligible response or a disproportionate power surge. Once neutron reproduction reinforces the initial transition, the reactor may move rapidly toward a new critical state. A reactor physicist can often predict the direction of change more easily than its exact timing, because hidden fissile inventory and neutron fluctuation obscure the approach to the criticality threshold.”

**Form (b):**  
“A reactor becomes critical when each generation of neutrons produces enough subsequent fissions to sustain the chain. A neutron absorbed by a fissile nucleus can therefore initiate a multiplying sequence through neighboring nuclei, converting nuclear binding energy into rapidly increasing power. Multiplication, generation time, and neutron leakage determine whether the sequence dies out or grows sharply. Neutron reproduction can drive the reactor quickly into a critical regime. Reactor physicists can generally determine whether reactivity is rising without predicting the exact instant of the transition, because fissile inventory and stochastic neutron behavior are imperfectly known.”

### Forest succession

**Form (a):**  
“A pioneer forest occupies disturbed soil by converting sunlight into canopy, but its own success gradually changes the conditions that supported it. Litter then increases shading and reduces light access, creating an opening for a shade-tolerant tree with a different shade-capture strategy. The transition is mediated by fire, which removes, redistributes, or exposes the seed bank and allows seed dispersal to begin. Ash from the earlier forest can either inhibit or enable the successor, so succession is neither simple replacement nor complete reset. A forest ecologist distinguishes seral stages by tracking resource flows, structural change, and the changing relation between the forest and disturbed soil.”

**Form (b):**  
“Pioneer trees colonize disturbed ground by turning sunlight into canopy, but canopy development eventually alters the light regime that favored the pioneers. Accumulated litter and shade reduce illumination near the ground, allowing shade-tolerant species to establish with a different strategy. Fire can reset or rearrange the site, exposing seed banks and enabling renewed dispersal. Ash may suppress some recruits and nourish others, so succession carries history forward rather than starting from nothing. Forest ecologists identify seral stages by following resources, vegetation structure, and the forest’s changing relationship with its site.”

**Form (a):**

Organizational complexity then increases coordination cost and reduces market access, creating an opening for an entrant firm with a different specialization strategy. The transition is mediated by bankruptcy, which removes, redistributes, or exposes the customer relationship and allows hiring to begin. Debt from the earlier firm can either inhibit or enable the successor, so succession is neither simple replacement nor complete reset. A management scholar distinguishes corporate life-cycle stages by tracking resource flows, structural change, and the changing relation between the firm and the market segment.”

**Form (b):**  
“An incumbent builds a customer base by serving existing demand, but the organization created by that success can become cumbersome. Complexity raises coordination costs and makes the market easier for a specialized entrant to reach. Bankruptcy may release customers, talent, and assets, allowing a successor to recruit and reorganize them. The predecessor’s debt can burden the new firm or make assets available cheaply, so corporate change is not a clean replacement. Management scholars analyse life-cycle stages through resources, organizational structure, and the firm’s changing position in its market.”

**Form (b):**

The transition is mediated by an antibiotic course, which removes, redistributes, or exposes microbial DNA and allows colonization to begin. **Form (b):**  
“Gut microbes colonize the intestinal lumen by converting dietary substrates into a biofilm and metabolic products, but their growth changes the chemical environment that sustained them. Accumulated metabolites can stress the community and deplete available substrates, opening space for anaerobes with different fermentative strategies. An antibiotic course removes some organisms, redistributes ecological opportunity, and exposes genetic material that can support recolonization. Resistance inherited from the earlier community may hinder or facilitate the next community, so recovery is not a clean replacement. Microbiologists distinguish community states by following substrates, metabolites, structure, and the microbes’ changing relation to the gut environment.”
