## Archetype Proposal: Selective Boundary

### Context-template

"A [SYSTEM] persists by maintaining a [BOUNDARY] that is selectively permeable to [FLOW]. The [BOUNDARY] admits [INPUT] needed for [FUNCTION] and expels [WASTE] that would otherwise impair it. Because unrestricted [FLOW] can also carry [THREAT], the [SYSTEM] uses [GATEKEEPER] to distinguish acceptable from unacceptable passage. If [GATEKEEPER] becomes too permissive, [THREAT] enters; if it becomes too restrictive, necessary [INPUT] is denied. [REGULATION] therefore concerns not eliminating exchange, but tuning the [BOUNDARY] so that exchange remains compatible with [VIABILITY]."

### Cell Membrane

**Form (a):**  
"A cell persists by maintaining a membrane that is selectively permeable to molecules. The membrane admits nutrients needed for metabolism and expels metabolic waste that would otherwise impair it. Because unrestricted molecular flow can also carry toxins, the cell uses transport proteins to distinguish acceptable from unacceptable passage. If transport proteins become too permissive, toxins enter; if they become too restrictive, necessary nutrients are denied. Membrane transport therefore concerns not eliminating exchange, but tuning the membrane so that exchange remains compatible with survival."

**Form (b):**  
"Cell survival depends on selective membrane permeability. Membranes import nutrients for metabolism and export metabolic wastes, while transport proteins discriminate among molecules because indiscriminate passage would admit toxins. Excessively permissive transport permits toxic entry, whereas excessively restrictive transport starves the cell of needed nutrients. Membrane transport is thus a problem of calibrated exchange rather than isolation."

### National Border

**Form (a):**  
"A country persists by maintaining a border that is selectively permeable to travelers. The border admits visitors needed for social life and expels unwanted burdens that would otherwise impair it. Because unrestricted traveler flow can also carry hostile actors, the country uses border officers to distinguish acceptable from unacceptable passage. If border officers become too permissive, hostile actors enter; if they become too restrictive, necessary visitors are denied. Border policy therefore concerns not eliminating exchange, but tuning the border so that exchange remains compatible with sovereignty."

**Form (b):**  
"A sovereign state requires a border that permits some movement while screening it. Visitors can support social and economic life, while authorities remove entrants whose continued presence imposes unacceptable burdens; yet open movement can also be exploited by hostile actors. Overly lax screening compromises security, while overly severe screening excludes legitimate visitors. Border policy therefore seeks calibrated openness consistent with sovereignty, not total closure."

### Computer Network

**Form (a):**  
"A network persists by maintaining a firewall that is selectively permeable to packets. The firewall admits legitimate traffic needed for service and expels malicious traffic that would otherwise impair it. Because unrestricted packet flow can also carry attacks, the network uses security rules to distinguish acceptable from unacceptable passage. If security rules become too permissive, attacks enter; if they become too restrictive, necessary legitimate traffic is denied. Access control therefore concerns not eliminating exchange, but tuning the firewall so that exchange remains compatible with reliability."

**Form (b):**  
"Reliable networks depend on firewalls that selectively admit packets. Legitimate traffic is necessary to provide service, whereas malicious traffic must be rejected; unrestricted connectivity would also expose the network to attack. Rules that are too permissive allow intrusions, while rules that are too restrictive block valid traffic. Access control is therefore the calibration of connectivity against reliability, not the abolition of connectivity."

### Publishing House

**Form (a):**  
"A publisher persists by maintaining an editorial boundary that is selectively permeable to manuscripts. The editorial boundary admits viable submissions needed for publication and expels unusable submissions that would otherwise impair it. Because unrestricted manuscript flow can also carry fraud, the publisher uses editors to distinguish acceptable from unacceptable passage. If editors become too permissive, fraud enters; if they become too restrictive, necessary viable submissions are denied. Editorial policy therefore concerns not eliminating exchange, but tuning the editorial boundary so that exchange remains compatible with solvency."

**Form (b):**  
"A publishing house needs an editorial filter rather than an open door or a sealed gate. Viable manuscripts sustain publication, while unusable submissions consume scarce editorial capacity; indiscriminate intake also creates opportunities for fraud. Editors who accept too readily compromise standards, but editors who reject too aggressively lose publishable work. Editorial policy therefore calibrates acquisition and screening in service of a solvent publishing program."

### Personal Relationship

**Form (a):**  
"A relationship persists by maintaining a boundary that is selectively permeable to disclosure. The boundary admits intimacy needed for trust and expels resentment that would otherwise impair it. Because unrestricted disclosure can also carry exploitation, the relationship uses judgment to distinguish acceptable from unacceptable passage. If judgment becomes too permissive, exploitation enters; if it becomes too restrictive, necessary intimacy is denied. Boundary-setting therefore concerns not eliminating exchange, but tuning the boundary so that exchange remains compatible with mutual well-being."

**Form (b):**  
"Healthy relationships require selective openness. Intimacy builds trust, and resentments must be addressed rather than retained, but indiscriminate disclosure can invite exploitation. Poor judgment can make a person dangerously exposed, whereas excessive guardedness prevents the intimacy on which trust depends. Boundary-setting is therefore the practice of regulating exchange for mutual well-being, not withdrawing from connection."
