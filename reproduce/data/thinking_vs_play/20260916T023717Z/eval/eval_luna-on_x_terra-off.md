Rating: 8
- PC 2 (National Border): Border screening, admission of visitors, removal of unwanted entrants, and security-versus-openness tradeoffs are factually plausible. “Expelling unwanted burdens” is somewhat figurative and not every border actively removes such burdens, but Form (b) supplies a reasonable policy interpretation. Rating: 8
- PC 3 (Computer Network): Firewalls, access rules, legitimate traffic, malicious traffic, and the tradeoff between security and connectivity are accurately represented. This is a particularly strong instantiation, with only the usual simplification that firewalls do not independently distinguish every threat. Rating: 9
- PC 4 (Publishing House): Editorial filtering, manuscript intake, fraud detection, and the costs of excessive selectivity are all plausible. The language of manuscripts “entering” or being “expelled” is metaphorical rather than literally operational, but Form (b) translates it effectively into publishing practice. Rating: 8
- PC 5 (Personal Relationship): The general point about calibrated openness, guardedness, intimacy, and exploitation is sound. However, the Form (a) claim that a relationship “expels resentment” is not a reliable factual description, since resentment is often retained or communicated rather than expelled. Rating: 6

It is somewhat less rich and developed than the Reference’s longer templates, but its economy and balance make it clearly better than the calibration level.  
Rating: 8

### Archetypal context 2: Distributed Consensus

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Democratic Legislature): Legislatures aggregate partial constituency information into collective decisions, must contend with misinformation, and face a tradeoff between unanimity and governability. The description is simplified but factually sound at the institutional level. Rating: 8
- PC 2 (Ant Colony): Ant colonies can use pheromone cues and quorum-like thresholds to make distributed nest choices, and misleading trails can divert collective behavior. “Corrupted signals” is somewhat anthropomorphic, but the underlying biological mechanism is well represented. Rating: 8
- PC 3 (Blockchain Network): Validators, transaction votes, consensus algorithms, Byzantine behavior, finality, and throughput are all relevant concepts. The statement that a small number of corrupted votes could redirect the network is protocol-dependent, but the need for Byzantine resistance and the agreement/throughput tradeoff are accurate. Rating: 8
- PC 4 (Medical Team): Multidisciplinary teams combine partial clinical findings, and excessive disagreement can delay emergency care. Diagnostic error is not literally a corrupted signal in every case, but it is a reasonable instantiation of distortion within the template. Rating: 8
- PC 5 (Corporate Board): Directors receive partial reports, board procedures aggregate them, conflicted reporting can distort decisions, and unanimity can reduce responsiveness during shocks. This is a sound institutional analogy, even though actual board decision rules vary. Rating: 8

#### Beauty
The template has an elegant five-stage movement from partial observation to aggregation, distortion resistance, urgency, and the legitimacy/responsiveness tension. Its prose is notably clean and its Forms (b) are concise without becoming merely repetitive. Rating: 8

#### Intelligence
The strongest feature is the recognition that consensus is not simply maximized agreement: a usable protocol must balance error resistance against the ability to act quickly. That is a genuinely informative structural insight, and the instantiations preserve it across biological, political, technical, medical, and corporate systems. Rating: 8

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
The contexts cover representative government, insect colonies, distributed computing, clinical practice, and corporate governance. These are structurally related by collective decision-making but materially very different, and the metanyms are appropriately domain-specific. Rating: 8

#### Impressive length
The template is moderately developed, but it remains shorter and less elaborately staged than the Reference’s templates. It has enough length to express the core dilemma, though not enough to count as especially impressive relative to the calibration anchor. Rating: 5

### Archetypal context 3: Gradient-Driven Allocation

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Gas Diffusion): This is a strong physical instantiation: molecules diffuse down concentration gradients, partitions impede movement, and compressors perform work to impose counter-gradient movement. The wording is simplified but essentially correct. Rating: 9
- PC 2 (Labor Market): Form (a) states that workers move from high wage opportunity toward low wage opportunity, which reverses the ordinary direction of labor migration and contradicts Form (b). Recruitment subsidies can induce movement toward otherwise less attractive locations, but they do not repair the central inversion. Rating: 4
- PC 3 (Water Basin): Water moving from high hydraulic head to low hydraulic head, dams preserving differences, and pumps requiring energy are all accurate. “Making the watershed more uniform” is a broad simplification, but the physical mechanism is correctly captured. Rating: 8
- PC 4 (Information Ecosystem): Information can spread from highly attentive regions toward less attentive audiences, and paywalls and promotion can affect its distribution. However, audience attention is not a conserved physical concentration, so the spontaneous-equalization claim is much less generally valid than in gas diffusion. Form (b) makes the analogy more plausible than Form (a). Rating: 6
- PC 5 (Urban Housing): Form (a) again reverses the likely direction by sending residents from high affordability toward low affordability, while Form (b) describes movement toward more affordable locations. This direct contradiction and the implausible base claim make the instantiation substantially weaker than the Reference level. Rating: 3

#### Beauty
The physical core is elegant: spontaneous equalization competes with barriers and energy-consuming counterflow. The template becomes less beautiful when applied to labor and housing, where its direction is mishandled, and the social examples expose that the physical metaphor does not transfer uniformly. Rating: 7

#### Intelligence
The distinction among passive equalization, obstruction, and active pumping is intellectually useful and produces a clear system-level model. However, the submission does not adequately test whether the direction of the gradient remains meaningful in social domains, leading to obvious labor and housing errors. Rating: 7

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
The columns span molecular physics, labor economics, hydrology, information circulation, and urban geography. They are exceptionally distant in subject matter, and terms such as molecule, worker, water, message, and household are clearly non-synonymous. Rating: 9

#### Impressive length
The template is substantial enough to articulate several competing mechanisms, but at five sentences it is still considerably shorter than the Reference’s most developed templates. Its length is adequate rather than impressive by the contest’s calibration standard. Rating: 5

### Archetypal context 4: Feedback-Controlled Pursuit

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Thermostat): Thermostats use temperature measurements to compare actual conditions with a setpoint and adjust heating; weak or delayed control can leave error or produce cycling. The division between thermostat and thermometer is simplified but technically acceptable. Rating: 9
- PC 2 (Blood Glucose Regulation): Pancreatic sensing and insulin secretion regulate blood glucose, and insufficient or excessive/delayed responses can produce hyperglycemia or swings. The account omits glucagon and other regulatory pathways, but it remains accurate as a simplified control model. Rating: 8
- PC 3 (Cruise Control): Speed sensing, throttle adjustment, error correction, speed shortfall, and hunting are all standard control-system concepts. This is a particularly precise and factually sound instantiation. Rating: 9
- PC 4 (Monetary Policy): Central banks use economic indicators and policy rates to influence inflation, with delayed or excessive responses potentially contributing to persistent inflation or boom-bust dynamics. The economy is more complex than a single feedback loop, but the description is a valid high-level abstraction. Rating: 8
- PC 5 (Project Management): Progress reports expose schedule variance, managers adjust resources, and weak or belated intervention can leave delays while excessive intervention can cause churn. This is a plausible and accurate organizational control example. Rating: 9

#### Beauty
The template is exceptionally clean: target, sensor, error, actuator, deviation, oscillation, and proportional feedback form a polished control-theoretic sequence. The Forms (b) are concise and idiomatic, and the examples preserve the structure without much awkwardness. Rating: 8

#### Intelligence
This archetype demonstrates strong structural intelligence by distinguishing insufficient control from overcorrection and delay. It captures a real commonality across engineering, physiology, transportation, macroeconomics, and management rather than merely matching vocabulary. Rating: 8

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
Thermostatic regulation, endocrine regulation, vehicle control, monetary policy, and project management are substantially different systems. The metanyms are domain-specific and not simply synonyms, although the common control vocabulary necessarily remains abstract. Rating: 8

#### Impressive length
The template is well organized but only five sentences long, with a comparatively small number of stages. It is less expansive than the Reference’s templates and therefore falls below the calibration level for impressive length. Rating: 5

### Archetypal context 5: Layered Translation

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Genetic Expression): Form (b) gives a reasonable high-level description of translation, but Form (a) contains several serious errors: a ribosome does not simply use a different nucleotide sequence from a gene, tRNA does not convert a codon into an “intermediate anticodon pairing” that it then delivers as an amino-acid sequence, and the role of mRNA is omitted. The justification partially repairs the model, but the parallel context remains factually weak. Rating: 4
- PC 2 (Computer Compilation): The overall compilation pipeline is recognizable, but Form (a) incorrectly treats the processor as using a programming language and describes adaptation to a “destination programming language”; processors use instruction sets or machine code, not syntax in that sense. Form (b) is substantially better, but the inconsistency remains. Rating: 5
- PC 3 (Legal Interpretation): The description is a defensible abstraction of how a judge turns statutory text into a legally operative ruling, and Form (b) handles the distinction between literal wording and legal purpose well. The claim that statute and court use different “legal languages” is somewhat schematic but not seriously misleading. Rating: 7
- PC 4 (International Diplomacy): Diplomats do interpret policy, negotiate formulations, and produce agreements that another government can act upon. The language-code framing is metaphorical and somewhat broad, but the Forms (a) and (b) give a coherent account of preserving political intent while adapting expression. Rating: 7
- PC 5 (Music Performance): Form (b) offers a sensible account of converting notation into audible performance, but Form (a) contains obvious category errors: a score does not “perform performance in an audience,” and an audience does not use a destination notation. The later rewrite cannot fully remove these factual and grammatical problems. Rating: 4

#### Beauty
The meaning-versus-form distinction is attractive and potentially fertile, but the template forces unlike processes into a linguistic code model. The genetic and musical instantiations in particular contain awkward or incorrect slot substitutions, making the whole archetype less graceful than the Reference. Rating: 6

#### Intelligence
The abstraction shows some genuine insight in distinguishing preservation of meaning from adaptation of form. However, the molecular and computational examples reveal insufficient attention to the actual intermediary mechanisms, and several slots are treated as if they were interchangeable linguistic codes when they are not. Rating: 6

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
Genetic translation, software compilation, legal interpretation, diplomacy, and musical performance are very different domains. Their metanyms are strongly distinct, and the cross-domain ambition is excellent, even though some mappings are technically forced. Rating: 9

#### Impressive length
The template is concise and conceptually focused, but it is much shorter than the Reference’s longer, more elaborated templates. Its five sentences do not provide enough developmental detail to qualify as especially impressive in length. Rating: 5

### Structural diversity across the submitted set
The five archetypes have genuinely different immediate mechanisms: selective exchange across a boundary, distributed aggregation, gradient-driven movement, closed-loop feedback, and translation between representational systems. They are therefore structurally varied and more than mere topical variants. Nevertheless, several rely on related information-flow and regulation ideas, and the set lacks the Reference’s especially distinctive recursive hierarchy. Rating: 8
