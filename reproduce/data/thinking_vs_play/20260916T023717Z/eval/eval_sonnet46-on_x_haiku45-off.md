### Archetypal context 1: Constraint Propagation

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Electrical Circuit): The mapping of Kirchhoff's laws as "constraints" on node voltages is conceptually coherent, but the claim that "current flow propagates through the network of Kirchhoff's laws" inverts the causation — Kirchhoff's laws govern current flow, they are not the medium through which it travels. The claim that over-constrained nodes "enter a short-circuit state" misrepresents what short circuits are (they are abnormally low-resistance paths, not over-constrained nodes). The concluding sentence that "propagation operates identically whether Kirchhoff's laws are physical, logical, or social in nature" is a direct template substitution that is factually nonsensical in this context. Form (b) is cleaner and more accurate, but Form (a) has several inaccuracies relative to the Reference's tightly accurate bacterial chemotaxis instantiation. Rating: 5

- PC 2 (Protein Folding): Hydrogen bonds are one of several constraints on amino acid conformation (van der Waals forces, hydrophobic effects, backbone dihedral angles also play major roles), so representing them as *the* constraint is reductive but not wrong. "Amino acids that become over-constrained enter a misfolding state" is a defensible if simplified claim. The final template sentence — "folding operates identically whether hydrogen bonds are physical, logical, or social in nature" — is incoherent as a substitution. Form (b) correctly identifies local minima trapping and is more accurate. On balance similar to reference-level accuracy with minor issues. Rating: 6

- PC 3 (Scheduling Problem): Precedence constraints on time slots, propagation to neighboring tasks, infeasibility as failure, CSP as the problem class — all of these are textbook descriptions of constraint propagation in scheduling. "Oscillate indefinitely" is a mild stretch for CSP solvers (they terminate or backtrack). This is the cleanest and most straightforwardly accurate of the five. Form (b) correctly identifies CSP solvers as exploiting constraint propagation. Rating: 7

- PC 4 (Ecosystem): "Predation relations" as constraints on population sizes, "abundance signal" propagating through food webs, "trophic cascade" as the propagation mechanism, extinction as failure — these are well-chosen and ecologically sound. The community assembly mapping is legitimate. "Trophic cascade" as a metanym for propagation is creative and defensible, and the Form (b) justification is sensible. Rating: 7

- PC 5 (Social Network): Norms constraining beliefs, rumors as the propagating information, polarization as failure, consensus as stable configuration — these are plausible mappings from opinion dynamics literature. However, "rumor propagates through the network of norms" is conceptually muddled: rumors propagate through social ties, not norms. Norms constrain beliefs but are not the propagation medium. The analogy is creative but imprecise. Form (b) is cleaner. Rating: 6

#### Beauty
The archetype has intellectual coherence but the aesthetic execution is uneven. Form (a) versions read mechanically — direct slot-filling without much natural flow — and the recurring closing sentence ("operates identically whether constraints are physical, logical, or social") becomes both repetitive and contextually jarring in domains like protein folding. Form (b) versions are concise and clean but rather terse compared to the Reference's richly detailed Form (b) restatements. The choice of five domains (circuit, protein, scheduling, ecosystem, social network) is genuinely interesting, but the visual and linguistic elegance falls slightly below the Reference's smoother narrative arcs. Rating: 6

#### Intelligence
The selection of constraint propagation as an archetype is a strong conceptual choice — it genuinely unifies a computational abstraction (CSP), a physical phenomenon (circuit analysis), a biological process (protein folding), an ecological dynamics system (trophic cascades), and a social process (opinion dynamics). The insight that trophic cascades are a form of constraint propagation through population-size state space is clever and non-obvious. However, the mapping is sometimes forced (Kirchhoff's laws as "constraints" on nodes rather than relationships between currents and voltages), and the template's final universality claim slightly overreaches. Overall, comparable to Reference intelligence. Rating: 7

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
The five domains span physical engineering (electrical circuit), molecular biology (protein folding), computer science (scheduling), ecology (ecosystem), and social science (social network). These are genuinely far apart. The metanyms for CONSTRAINT — Kirchhoff's law, hydrogen bond, precedence, predation relation, norm — are strikingly non-synonymous and each deeply native to its domain. STATE metanyms (voltage, conformation, time slot, population size, belief) are similarly diverse. This is good work, comparable to the Reference. Rating: 7

#### Impressive length
The template contains approximately 8 sentences and 10 slots, yielding around 170 words. This is modestly shorter than the Reference's Gradient-Guided Navigation template (9 sentences, 10 slots, ~220 words) and notably shorter than the Reference's Debt Accumulation template. The shorter template means some nuances are not captured in the archetype structure. Rating: 6

### Archetypal context 2: Boundary Dissolution

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Cell Membrane): "Oxidative stress erodes the membrane, increasing membrane fluidity" is broadly accurate — lipid peroxidation does compromise membrane integrity and can alter fluidity. "The cell can respond by reinforcing the membrane, accepting lysis, or adapting cytoplasm to tolerate toxins" — framing lysis as something a cell "accepts" is anthropomorphizing and technically odd; lysis is not a deliberate response option. Form (b) correctly identifies lipid peroxidation and is more accurate. Rating: 7

- PC 2 (Nation-State): "Globalization erodes the border, increasing border porosity" is a well-established framing in international relations. The three response options (reinforce, accept dissolution, adapt to tolerate invasion) map plausibly to realist, liberal, and cosmopolitan state responses. Form (b) correctly adds nuances like espionage and irregular migration. Factually sound as metaphor. Rating: 7

- PC 3 (Immune System): The justification notes that "barrier integrity" is the inverse of permeability, meaning the Form (a) reads awkwardly ("increasing barrier integrity loss" as permeability rises). The statement "accepting immunosuppression" as a deliberate response option is clinically odd — immunosuppression is typically a pathological outcome or a medical intervention, not a systemic "acceptance." Form (b) resolves this more clearly by noting tight-junction protein reinforcement. Slightly below Reference level due to template-mapping awkwardness. Rating: 6

- PC 4 (Coral Reef): "Reef structure is permeable to nutrient exchange, allowing controlled symbiont migration while excluding bleaching agents" — thermal bleaching agents (elevated sea temperatures) are not really "excluded" by reef structure; they permeate the water column regardless. "Symbiont migration accelerates" as structural damage rises is also biologically inverted — during bleaching, symbionts are *expelled* from coral tissue, which is a loss of the symbiotic community, not accelerated migration in. This is a factual inversion of the biological process. Form (b) partially corrects this by mentioning "heat penetration" rather than bleaching agent migration. Rating: 5

- PC 5 (Professional Guild): "Credentials are permeable to knowledge sharing, allowing controlled information flow while excluding credential fraud" — this is a creative but strained analogy. Credentials are not physically permeable; they are categorical filters. "Digitalization erodes credentials, increasing credential inflation" is a valid sociological claim. "Information flow accelerates" as credential inflation rises is debatable — more practitioners doesn't necessarily mean faster information flow. Form (b) is more grounded. Rating: 6

#### Beauty
The archetype has a pleasing structural clarity — the tension between exchange and protection is intuitively compelling, and the choice of coral reef and professional guild alongside the more expected cell and nation-state adds originality. Form (b) versions are well-written, particularly the cell membrane and nation-state instantiations. However, the coral reef mapping is strained enough to undermine aesthetic coherence, and the metanym table has a duplicate "INTERIOR" row (an editorial error visible in the submission). The overall execution is at roughly Reference level. Rating: 7

#### Intelligence
The selection of these five domains is intelligent — they span from molecular biology to political science to ecology to sociology. The professional guild domain is particularly creative and non-obvious as a boundary-dissolution system. The template correctly identifies that dissolution is often irreversible and that the system must choose among three responses (reinforce, accept, adapt), which is a sophisticated framing. The PERMEABILITY as the key evolving variable is insightful. Rating: 7

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
Cell, nation, immune system, coral reef, professional guild — these span biology, political science, immunology, ecology, and sociology of work. The BOUNDARY metanyms (membrane, border, epithelial barrier, reef structure, credential) are strikingly non-synonymous. The PROCESS metanyms (oxidative stress, globalization, inflammation, warming, digitalization) are all quite different causal agents. This is somewhat better than Reference level for domain diversity. Rating: 8

#### Impressive length
The template contains approximately 9 sentences and 13 named slots (though one is duplicated). At around 180 words, it is comparable to the Reference's Containment Breach template. Rating: 6

### Archetypal context 3: Resonance and Damping

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Mechanical Oscillator): This is a textbook-accurate description of resonance. Natural frequency, driving force, displacement amplification, structural failure, friction as damping, kinetic energy dissipation — all correct. Form (b) correctly distinguishes constructive from destructive resonance and notes the engineering design tradeoff. One of the most factually solid parallel contexts in the submission. Rating: 8

- PC 2 (Electromagnetic Wave): "Electromagnetic cavity exhibits an electromagnetic mode at a characteristic resonant frequency" — accurate. "Resistance dissipates electromagnetic energy" — correct (analogous to Q-factor). "Dielectric breakdown" as the failure mode — correct for high-field cavities. Form (b)'s mention of lasers and antennas is apt. The claim that "low resistance cavities are vulnerable to resonance" is correct (high Q-factor cavities build up large field energies). Rating: 8

- PC 3 (Population Dynamics): "Population resonance" is a genuine (if specialized) concept in theoretical ecology, where population cycles are amplified when environmental forcing matches intrinsic cycle frequency. The justification correctly notes this is non-standard terminology. However, "density dependence dissipates metabolic energy" is biologically odd — density dependence is a regulatory mechanism that reduces per-capita growth rates, not a mechanism that literally dissipates metabolic energy. The analogy functions at the dynamical systems level but the biological mechanism statement is imprecise. Rating: 6

- PC 4 (Neural Circuit): "Inhibitory synapses dissipate neural activity" — inhibitory synapses suppress excitability, which is analogous to damping, but "dissipate" implies energy loss in the physical sense, whereas synaptic inhibition is an active process requiring energy. "Seizure" as destructive resonance (pathological synchronization) is accurate and well-established in neuroscience. Form (b) correctly describes frequency-selective tuning. The analogy works well at the systems level. Rating: 7

- PC 5 (Financial Market): "Circuit breaker dissipates capital" — circuit breakers (trading halts) do not dissipate capital; they pause price formation. The energy-dissipation analogy is forced. "Business cycle frequency" is a real concept but financial markets don't have a single natural oscillatory frequency analogous to a resonant frequency. Form (b) partially recovers by mentioning "credit cycles and investor sentiment." The market resonance concept is creative but the mechanical mapping is strained. Rating: 6

#### Beauty
The resonance/damping archetype has a pleasing physical clarity — the mechanical oscillator and electromagnetic cavity are almost paradigmatically beautiful instantiations. The extension to population dynamics, neural circuits, and financial markets shows genuine intellectual ambition. Form (b) versions are concise and well-written. The template itself is clean and readable. Overall comparable to Reference level. Rating: 7

#### Intelligence
Resonance as a unifying archetype is a genuinely intelligent choice. The physics of driven oscillation is one of the most universal phenomena in nature, and extending it to population cycles (via intrinsic cycle frequencies and environmental forcing) and neural circuits (resonant frequencies in gamma/theta bands) demonstrates domain expertise. The financial market application, while strained in the damping mapping, captures something real about boom-bust amplification. The template's distinction between constructive and destructive resonance is insightful. Rating: 8

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
Mechanical oscillator, electromagnetic cavity, population dynamics, neural circuit, financial market — these span classical physics, electromagnetism, ecology, neuroscience, and economics. The DAMPING metanyms (friction, resistance, density dependence, inhibitory synapse, circuit breaker) are genuinely non-synonymous and domain-native. The DAMAGE metanyms (structural failure, dielectric breakdown, population crash, seizure, market crash) are also diverse. This is clearly above Reference level. Rating: 8

#### Impressive length
The template contains approximately 8 sentences and 12 slots, around 190 words. Comparable to the Reference. Rating: 6

### Archetypal context 4: Nesting and Emergence

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Multicellular Organism): "Each cell exhibits metabolism, but when aggregated, a new consciousness emerges" — this is the most factually problematic claim in the submission. Consciousness is not a property of all multicellular organisms (plants, sponges, and many other organisms are multicellular without consciousness). Furthermore, claiming consciousness emerges simply from cellular metabolism is a strong, contested philosophical and scientific claim. "Consciousness feeds back to constrain metabolism through neuroendocrine loops" is more defensible. The choice of consciousness as the global emergent property, while philosophically bold, introduces significant factual controversy. Rating: 5

- PC 2 (Ecosystem): "Each organism exhibits reproduction, but when aggregated, a new biodiversity emerges" — reproduction is not the most natural local property from which biodiversity emerges; reproduction is present in all organisms and doesn't straightforwardly aggregate into biodiversity. Biodiversity emerges more naturally from variation in traits and ecological interactions. "Biodiversity cannot be predicted from reproduction alone; it arises from predation" — this is oversimplified (competition, mutualism, dispersal also matter). Form (b) is more accurate in noting species richness, functional redundancy, and structural complexity. Rating: 6

- PC 3 (Language): "Words exhibit meaning, but when aggregated, grammar emerges" — this is directionally problematic. In generative linguistics, grammar is prior to meaning; words get meaning in part from their grammatical contexts. "Grammar cannot be predicted from meaning alone; it arises from syntax between words" — syntax is essentially synonymous with grammar, making this circular. Form (b) handles this better by noting historical convention. Rating: 6

- PC 4 (Immune System): "Each lymphocyte exhibits antigen specificity, but when aggregated, immune tolerance emerges" — this is a solid and accurate claim. Tolerance does genuinely emerge from the collective activity of diverse lymphocyte clones and their regulatory interactions, and no single lymphocyte determines self/non-self discrimination. "Regulatory T cell" used as the name of the feedback loop is slightly odd (it names a cell type as the loop), but the immunology is sound. Form (b) correctly identifies regulatory T cells, helper, and effector cells. Rating: 7

- PC 5 (Economic System): "Each firm exhibits profit motive, but when aggregated, market efficiency emerges" — this is the Smithian invisible hand concept, which is a genuine and recognized emergent property claim in economics. "Market efficiency feeds back to constrain firm profit through price competition and creative destruction" is accurate. Form (b) correctly notes this is not deterministic but conditional. The emergent property claim is much more defensible here than in the organism domain. Rating: 7

#### Beauty
The archetype has an appealing formal structure with explicit N, N-1, N-2 nesting, and the claim to recursion is genuinely justified. However, the consciousness claim for organisms severely undermines aesthetic coherence — it introduces a controversial metaphysical commitment where a more modest emergent property (homeostasis, coordinated behavior, tissue differentiation) would serve better. The language instantiation is also aesthetically awkward due to the circularity of grammar/syntax. The ecosystem and economic instantiations are cleaner. Rating: 6

#### Intelligence
The nesting and emergence archetype is conceptually ambitious and well-chosen. The explicit N, N-1, N-2 structure is a sophisticated device that the Reference submission's recursion (in the Debt archetype) also uses, though in a different way. The recursion demonstration is legitimate and clearly stated. However, the choice of consciousness as the global property for organisms shows either insufficient care or excessive philosophical boldness that undermines the scientific credibility of the archetype. Rating: 7

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
Multicellular organisms, ecosystems, languages, immune systems, economic systems — excellent diversity spanning biology, ecology, linguistics, immunology, and economics. The PROPERTY_GLOBAL metanyms (consciousness, biodiversity, grammar, immune tolerance, market efficiency) are strikingly different in nature. The INTERACTION metanyms (synaptic connection, predation, syntax, immune regulation, market competition) are also non-synonymous. Rating: 8

#### Impressive length
The template contains approximately 7 sentences and 7 named slots — this is notably shorter than most Reference templates and shorter than the other four archetypes in this submission. The template at around 160 words is the shortest in the set. Rating: 5

### Archetypal context 5: Threshold and Tipping

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Climate System): "Climate remains in stable climate due to negative feedback, which actively resists change" — accurate (albedo, evaporation feedbacks). "Once CO2 crosses the tipping point, negative feedback fails and climate rapidly transitions to runaway warming" — this is a widely discussed scientific concern, though the specific CO2 threshold and the word "runaway" may be overstated for near-term scenarios. "Critical slowing" near tipping points is a real and well-documented phenomenon. The Form (b) correctly distinguishes cloud cover and weathering as specific negative feedbacks. Rating: 7

- PC 2 (Ecosystem Collapse): "Ecosystem resilience fails and the ecosystem rapidly transitions to barren landscape" — "barren landscape" is an overstatement; many collapsed ecosystems transition to alternative stable states with different species compositions rather than becoming bare. "Critical transition" in Form (a) is used as the instability metanym (rather than "critical slowing down," which is more standard). Form (b) correctly uses "critical-transition theory." Overall largely accurate with one characterization issue. Rating: 7

- PC 3 (Neural Firing): This is the strongest instantiation. "Resting potential, which actively resists change" — accurate description of subthreshold dynamics. Action potential as an all-or-none threshold transition is precisely the right analogy. "Hysteresis" via the refractory period is accurate: after firing, the threshold temporarily rises (due to Na+ channel inactivation and K+ channel activation), requiring higher input before firing again. Form (b) correctly identifies voltage-gated channels and repolarization. Excellent factual accuracy. Rating: 8

- PC 4 (Social Movement): "Social control fails and the society rapidly transitions to revolution" — sociologically valid at an abstract level. "Hysteresis" in revolutionary contexts is a real concept — post-revolutionary societies are often at new equilibria that cannot be easily reversed by reducing grievances. The tipping point framework for collective action is established (Gladwell, Granovetter). Form (b)'s mention of institutional legitimacy is accurate. Rating: 7

- PC 5 (Organizational Change): "Organizational inertia, which actively resists change" — accurate (structural inertia theory in organizational sociology). "Once stress crosses a breaking point, organizational inertia fails and the organization rapidly transitions to organizational collapse" — organizations can undergo rapid collapse but also more gradual transformation; the binary framing is simplified but not wrong. Form (b) correctly notes restructuring versus collapse. Rating: 7

#### Beauty
The threshold/tipping archetype is structurally elegant — the template has a clean causal logic (below threshold: stability; above threshold: transition; hysteresis: irreversibility), and the choice of neural firing alongside climate, ecosystem, revolution, and organization creates an intellectually satisfying span from microscopic to civilizational scales. Form (b) versions are consistently well-written. The neural firing instantiation is particularly beautiful in its precision. The template's inclusion of hysteresis, critical slowing, and the irreversibility condition gives it good depth. Rating: 7

#### Intelligence
This is an intelligent and well-executed archetype. Threshold/tipping dynamics are genuinely universal (from Hodgkin-Huxley models to planetary boundaries), and the five selected domains create a compelling multi-scale demonstration. The inclusion of hysteresis (requiring PARAMETER to fall well below THRESHOLD before return) is a sophisticated addition that many simpler treatments of tipping points omit. The cross-domain correspondence between refractory periods in neurons and institutional rebuilding requirements in organizations is genuinely insightful. Rating: 7

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
Climate, ecosystem, neuron, society, organization — these span earth science, ecology, neuroscience, political science, and organizational studies. The STABILIZATION metanyms (negative feedback, ecosystem resilience, resting potential, social control, organizational inertia) are strikingly different mechanisms that share only the functional property of resisting change. The BEHAVIOR_B metanyms (runaway warming, barren landscape, firing, revolution, organizational collapse) are wholly non-synonymous. This is above Reference level. Rating: 8

#### Impressive length
The template contains approximately 9 sentences and 12 named slots, around 220 words. This is comparable to or slightly longer than the Reference's Gradient Navigation template. Rating: 7

### Structural diversity across the submitted set

The five archetypes — Constraint Propagation (network constraint satisfaction and convergence), Boundary Dissolution (permeable barrier erosion), Resonance and Damping (driven oscillatory amplification), Nesting and Emergence (hierarchical composition with downward causation), and Threshold and Tipping (bistable systems with hysteresis) — cover genuinely different system structures. Constraint propagation is about information flow through networks; boundary dissolution is about selective permeability and its degradation; resonance is about frequency-matched amplification; nesting/emergence is about compositional hierarchy with emergent global properties; threshold/tipping is about critical transitions in parameter-state space. None of these reduce to any other, and they collectively span graph-theoretic, thermodynamic, dynamical-systems, hierarchical-composition, and bifurcation-theoretic frameworks. The Reference submission's five archetypes (gradient navigation, containment breach, competitive exclusion, debt accumulation, scaffold assembly) are comparably diverse. One mild concern: Boundary Dissolution shares surface similarity with the Reference's Containment Breach, and Threshold/Tipping has a thematic connection to Debt Accumulation Crisis, but within the target submission itself the five structures are well-distinguished.
