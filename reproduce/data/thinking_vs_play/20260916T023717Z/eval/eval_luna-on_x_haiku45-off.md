## Target Submission

### Archetypal context 1: Constraint Propagation

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Electrical Circuit): The circuit analogy is intelligible, and Kirchhoff’s laws do impose voltage and current constraints, but current does not literally propagate “through the network of laws.” More seriously, an over-constrained node does not generally enter a short-circuit state, and the claim that the same propagation operates identically across physical, logical, and social systems is overstated. Compared with the Reference, this is somewhat less factually disciplined. Rating: 5
- PC 2 (Protein Folding): Hydrogen bonding does constrain conformation and local structural decisions can influence neighboring residues, but folding is governed by more than hydrogen bonds, and “over-constrained” amino acids do not literally enter a misfolding state. Form (b) improves the scientific account, though the indefinite-oscillation language remains speculative. Compared with the Reference, it is slightly weaker. Rating: 6
- PC 3 (Scheduling Problem): This is the strongest instantiation in this archetype: assigning a task can narrow the feasible slots of dependent tasks, and propagation can expose infeasibility. Constraint propagation does not itself predict completion time, and iterative solvers do not necessarily oscillate indefinitely, but the core account is sound. It is approximately comparable to the Reference. Rating: 7
- PC 4 (Ecosystem): Predation can transmit abundance changes through food webs and produce stable, collapsing, or oscillatory dynamics. However, predation is not literally a hard constraint on population size, and “over-constrained” species entering extinction is a loose metaphor rather than a general ecological mechanism. The final claim of identical operation across physical, logical, and social constraints is also false. Rating: 6
- PC 5 (Social Network): Social norms can constrain publicly expressed beliefs, and rumor or influence can spread through social networks, but norms do not normally limit the possible beliefs of agents in the strict CSP sense. The relationship between rumor, norm propagation, and polarization is plausible but simplified. Relative to the Reference, this is somewhat less factually precise. Rating: 6

#### Beauty
The template has a clean abstract shape and the movement from local assignment to global convergence or failure is attractive. The parallel contexts are easy to recognize, but the repeated final claim of identical operation across physical, logical, and social systems and several forced substitutions make the prose less elegant than the Reference. Rating: 7

#### Intelligence
The submission identifies a genuinely powerful common structure linking circuit analysis, folding, scheduling, trophic dynamics, and opinion formation. Its abstraction is intellectually useful, although it sometimes treats loose analogies as literal identity and does not adequately distinguish constraint satisfaction from dynamical propagation. Overall it is about as intelligent as the Reference. Rating: 7

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
Electrical engineering, molecular biophysics, operations research, ecology, and social influence are substantially different domains, and most of the metanyms are domain-specific rather than synonyms. Some entries, such as “propagation,” “configuration,” and “outcome,” remain generic, but the cross-domain span is slightly stronger than the Reference’s comparable set. Rating: 8

#### Impressive length
The template is substantial, with eight sentences and a useful number of interacting slots, but it is shorter and less richly parameterized than the Reference’s longest templates. It is nevertheless clearly developed rather than merely schematic. Rating: 7

### Archetypal context 2: Boundary Dissolution

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Cell Membrane): Oxidative stress can damage membranes and increase permeability, and greater permeability can permit both nutrient movement and toxin entry. However, lysis is not really a response chosen by the cell, and “adapting cytoplasm to tolerate toxins” is vague. Form (b) is substantially better than Form (a), making the overall account slightly below the Reference. Rating: 6
- PC 2 (Nation-State): Globalization can increase border porosity and trade while also facilitating unwanted cross-border flows. The claims that globalization necessarily erodes borders and that nation-state collapse becomes inevitable below a critical threshold are much too strong, though Form (b) adds plausible examples. Rating: 6
- PC 3 (Immune System): This is weakened by the table’s missing [PROTECTION] row and the substitution “increasing barrier integrity loss,” which is semantically awkward because integrity loss is not the same thing as permeability. Epithelial barrier damage can promote pathogen penetration, but immunosuppression and immune-system collapse are not inevitable consequences. Rating: 5
- PC 4 (Coral Reef): Warming and bleaching are appropriately connected, and reef degradation can increase exposure to thermal stress. Nevertheless, reef structure is not simply a boundary that excludes a “bleaching agent,” symbiont migration need not accelerate with structural damage, and bleaching is not always irreversible. Rating: 5
- PC 5 (Professional Guild): The analogy is the least factual: credentials are not normally permeable membranes, knowledge sharing is not a flow through credentials, and digitalization does not straightforwardly produce professionalization collapse. Form (b) makes the intended concern about fraud and gatekeeping clearer, but the underlying mapping remains strained. Rating: 5

#### Beauty
The boundary-and-permeability image is aesthetically strong and yields an immediately legible family of analogies. However, the omitted table slot, duplicated [INTERIOR] row, awkward immune substitution, and especially the professional-guild mapping noticeably reduce polish compared with the Reference. Rating: 6

#### Intelligence
The archetype insightfully connects membrane damage, border porosity, immune barriers, reef degradation, and credential erosion. Its intelligence is limited by treating all these processes as the same irreversible dissolution mechanism and by overlooking important domain-specific differences. It is somewhat less conceptually disciplined than the Reference. Rating: 6

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
Cells, states, immune systems, reefs, and professions are clearly different systems, and many metanyms are distinct. Still, the mappings are fairly literal and several terms are generic or semantically misaligned; the missing [PROTECTION] metanym also damages the table. Overall this is roughly comparable to the Reference rather than clearly better. Rating: 7

#### Impressive length
The template has eight substantial sentences and a broad set of slots, making it reasonably impressive in length. Its length is comparable to the Reference’s containment template, although the table defect makes the construction feel incomplete. Rating: 7

### Archetypal context 3: Resonance and Damping

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Mechanical Oscillator): The account of natural frequency, external driving, resonant amplitude growth, frictional damping, and structural failure is broadly correct. The distinction between constructive and destructive resonance is somewhat simplified, but this is a strong and technically recognizable instantiation, slightly better than the Reference’s average factual quality. Rating: 8
- PC 2 (Electromagnetic Wave): Resonant cavities support standing modes, external driving can amplify field strength, and resistive losses damp the response. The relation between cavity resistance, radiation emission, and dielectric breakdown is simplified, but the account is broadly defensible and comparable to the Reference. Rating: 7
- PC 3 (Population Dynamics): Ecological resonance is a legitimate modeling idea, but generation time is not simply a natural frequency, and density dependence does not literally dissipate metabolic energy. “Population design” and “population stability” are also forced substitutions. The justification appropriately admits that the terminology is nonstandard, but the factual quality remains below the Reference. Rating: 5
- PC 4 (Neural Circuit): Neural circuits can resonate at preferred frequencies, inhibition can damp oscillations, and excessive synchronization can contribute to seizures. Treating an inhibitory synapse as a scalar damping quantity and equating amplitude with firing rate is simplified, but Form (b) is domain-plausible. Rating: 7
- PC 5 (Financial Market): Financial markets can exhibit cyclical amplification and trading halts can reduce volatility, but a circuit breaker does not literally dissipate capital, and shocks do not generally resonate simply because they match a business-cycle frequency. “Market resonance” is a useful metaphor but not a settled mechanism. Rating: 5

#### Beauty
This is one of the more elegant templates: frequency matching, amplification, damping, and the tradeoff between responsiveness and stability form a coherent progression. The population and financial instantiations become strained, and the repeated use of “resonance” as the same word across domains reduces stylistic richness. Rating: 7

#### Intelligence
The archetype shows good conceptual ambition by transferring a precise physical mechanism into ecology, neuroscience, and finance. Its strongest insight is the tradeoff between beneficial amplification and destructive instability; its weakness is treating metaphorical resonance as if it had identical mechanics in every domain. Rating: 7

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
Mechanics, electromagnetism, population ecology, neuroscience, and finance span very different subject areas, and most system, driver, amplitude, and damping terms are domain-specific. The repeated literal metanym “resonance” and several near-generic entries prevent this from being clearly superior to the Reference, but the domain spread is strong. Rating: 7

#### Impressive length
The eight-sentence template is substantial and contains enough slots to express both the driving mechanism and its stabilizing counterforce. It is about as long and structurally developed as the comparable Reference templates. Rating: 7

### Archetypal context 4: Nesting and Emergence

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Multicellular Organism): Cells and organelles form a genuine nested hierarchy, and consciousness is plausibly treated as an emergent organism-level property associated with neural interactions. The claims that consciousness directly constrains metabolism and that stronger feedback necessarily reduces flexibility are broad generalizations, but the account is broadly defensible. Rating: 7
- PC 2 (Ecosystem): Ecosystems are hierarchically composed and biodiversity is an emergent system-level property. However, biodiversity does not arise from predation alone, and its feedback on reproduction is more indirect and context-dependent than the form suggests. The account is somewhat weaker than the Reference. Rating: 6
- PC 3 (Language): Phonemes can compose words and words participate in larger linguistic structures, but words do not universally consist only of phonemes, and grammar does not simply emerge from “syntax between words.” Form (b) offers a more credible account of grammatical emergence and semantic feedback, though the mapping remains simplified. Rating: 6
- PC 4 (Immune System): Immune tolerance can emerge from interactions among lymphocyte populations and regulatory cells, but an immune system is not composed only of lymphocytes, and a regulatory T cell is not itself a feedback loop. The core emergence claim is sound, while the specific substitutions are imprecise. Rating: 6
- PC 5 (Economic System): Firms and workers form nested economic structures, and price competition can produce aggregate allocation and efficiency effects not possessed by individual firms. Market efficiency is not guaranteed, and the stability-versus-flexibility claim is generalized, but the overall account is reasonably accurate. Rating: 7

#### Beauty
The explicit N, N−1, and N−2 structure is elegant, and the addition of downward feedback gives the archetype more depth than a simple “parts make wholes” analogy. The repeated template becomes somewhat mechanical, and several instantiations overstate emergence or feedback, but this is more aesthetically compelling than the Reference overall. Rating: 8

#### Intelligence
This is the submission’s strongest conceptual contribution. It explicitly identifies recursion, emergence, and downward causation across organisms, ecosystems, language, immunity, and economies. The claims that global properties cannot be predicted from local properties alone and that stronger feedback uniformly reduces flexibility are too absolute, but the organizing intelligence is clearly above the Reference. Rating: 8

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
The five instantiations span biology, ecology, linguistics, immunology, and economics. Their component properties and interaction terms are genuinely different rather than simple synonyms, while still preserving the nested-emergence pattern. This is clearly stronger than the Reference on this criterion. Rating: 8

#### Impressive length
The template is conceptually dense but only seven sentences long and has fewer slots than the Reference’s most elaborate recursive template. Its explicit scale notation adds sophistication, but the raw template length is somewhat below the Reference. Rating: 6

### Archetypal context 5: Threshold and Tipping

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Climate System): Climate tipping points, stabilizing feedbacks, critical slowing, irreversibility, and hysteresis are all legitimate concepts. The form overstates the existence of a single CO2 threshold and the inevitability of runaway warming, but Form (b) gives a credible high-level account. Rating: 7
- PC 2 (Ecosystem Collapse): Ecosystem resilience and threshold-driven collapse are well established, and restoration can exhibit hysteresis. The duplicated “critical critical threshold,” the shift from resource depletion to resource extraction, and the categorical claims about irreversibility reduce factual precision. Rating: 6
- PC 3 (Neural Firing): Threshold firing and subthreshold noise are appropriate, but an action potential is not an irreversible transition of the neuron, and the hysteresis claim confuses refractory effects with a requirement for current to fall below the original threshold. This is clearly weaker than the Reference. Rating: 5
- PC 4 (Social Movement): Grievance, social control, mobilization thresholds, and hysteresis-like recovery barriers can be useful political-science concepts. However, revolution is not generally irreversible, and the account treats a highly contingent process as a universal threshold system. Rating: 6
- PC 5 (Organizational Change): Organizational inertia, stress thresholds, fragility, and difficult recovery are plausible. Collapse is not necessarily inevitable or irreversible, and “hysteresis” is used more rhetorically than analytically. Rating: 6

#### Beauty
Threshold, resistance, tipping, irreversibility, and hysteresis form a familiar but effective dramatic progression. The template is weakened by repetitive substitutions, awkward phrases such as “stable climate,” and claims of inevitability that make the prose less nuanced than the Reference. Rating: 7

#### Intelligence
The archetype correctly brings together several sophisticated ideas—critical slowing, stabilizing feedback, hysteresis, and early-warning signals—rather than merely describing abrupt change. Its intelligence is reduced by applying the same bistable model too confidently to neurons, revolutions, organizations, and ecosystems. Rating: 8

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
Climate science, ecology, neuroscience, political science, and organizational theory are substantially different domains, and parameters and behaviors such as CO2 concentration, input current, grievance, and organizational stress are not synonyms. However, “irreversible” and “hysteresis” are reused identically across all five columns, and the first two domains are closely related. Rating: 7

#### Impressive length
The template contains eight sentences and a large set of slots covering stabilization, transition, reversibility, instability, hysteresis, and intervention. It is comparable in length and ambition to the Reference’s longer templates. Rating: 7

### Structural diversity across the submitted set
The submitted set has strong structural variety: constraint-network propagation, boundary erosion, driven oscillation, nested emergence, and threshold tipping represent genuinely different system organizations. The explicit recursive nesting archetype is a particular strength, although the Reference also contains a recursive hierarchy and a similarly broad structural range. Overall, the Target is somewhat better than the Reference on system-structure diversity. Rating: 8
