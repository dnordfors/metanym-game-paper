### Archetypal context 1: Selective Boundary

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Cell Membrane): The cell-membrane account is broadly accurate and clearly expressed. Transport proteins do regulate selective molecular passage, nutrient uptake, waste export, and toxin exclusion, although “expels metabolic waste” is a simplification because some waste crosses by diffusion or other transport mechanisms. Relative to the Reference, it is comparably sound but less technically nuanced. Rating: 7
- PC 2 (National Border): The border analogy is factually plausible: borders regulate movement, visitors can support social life, and screening can trade openness against security. “Expels unwanted burdens” is somewhat vague and countries do not literally require visitors for social life, but the claims remain defensible. Relative to the Reference, this is approximately equal in factual reliability. Rating: 7
- PC 3 (Computer Network): The network account is substantially correct. Firewalls filter packets, legitimate traffic supports services, and overly permissive or restrictive rules create opposing risks. Strictly, firewalls do not necessarily “expel” all malicious traffic or distinguish threats perfectly, but the simplification is acceptable. Rating: 8
- PC 4 (Publishing House): The publishing account is generally accurate: editorial screening admits viable manuscripts, rejects unusable ones, and can guard against fraud. The claim that viable submissions are “needed for publication” and that manuscript flow directly threatens solvency is somewhat schematic, but not materially false. Rating: 7
- PC 5 (Personal Relationship): The relationship account is a reasonable psychological and interpersonal generalization. Intimacy can support trust, resentment can impair relationships, and indiscriminate disclosure can create vulnerability, though “expelling resentment” is metaphorical rather than a literal relational process. Rating: 7

#### Beauty
The template is concise, balanced, and aesthetically coherent, with a strong central tension between openness and protection. The five instantiations preserve that symmetry effectively, and the rewrites are polished. Compared with the Reference’s longer, more elaborate prose, this is less richly textured but arguably cleaner and more elegant. Rating: 8

#### Intelligence
The archetype identifies a genuinely useful systems principle: viability depends not on isolation but on calibrated exchange. The explicit permissive-versus-restrictive failure modes and the gatekeeper distinction show good abstraction. It is somewhat less conceptually deep than the Reference’s more elaborate causal structures, but its compression is intelligent. Rating: 8

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
The domains span cell biology, geopolitics, cybersecurity, publishing, and intimate relationships, which are substantially different systems. The metanyms are mostly well chosen and not synonyms, though several mappings—especially “input,” “waste,” and “gatekeeper”—remain fairly generic across domains. The spread is slightly better than the Reference’s typical domain range. Rating: 8

#### Impressive length
The template is moderately long and contains a complete causal tradeoff, but it has only ten slots and seven sentences. It is shorter and less structurally elaborate than the Reference’s strongest templates. Rating: 6

### Archetypal context 2: Distributed Consensus

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Democratic Legislature): The account correctly describes representative institutions as aggregating partial information through procedures, with tradeoffs between robustness, legitimacy, and decisiveness. The claim that a small number of corrupted reports can redirect a legislature is context-dependent, but reasonable. Rating: 8
- PC 2 (Ant Colony): Ant colonies do use distributed cues and quorum-like mechanisms in collective nest selection, and waiting for unanimity could be maladaptive under threat. However, “corrupted pheromone cues” anthropomorphizes misleading trails, and the exact quorum mapping is simplified. Rating: 7
- PC 3 (Blockchain Network): The account is broadly accurate for consensus systems: validators have partial views, protocols tolerate Byzantine behavior, and stronger agreement can affect throughput. The relationship between complete agreement and throughput is not universal, but the formulation is defensible. Rating: 8
- PC 4 (Medical Team): Multidisciplinary teams integrate partial findings, diagnostic error can misdirect care, and excessive insistence on unanimity can delay emergency treatment. This is a sound high-level description, though “case conference” is not always the operative mechanism. Rating: 8
- PC 5 (Corporate Board): Boards aggregate partial reports and balance fiduciary legitimacy against speed, while conflicted reporting can distort decisions. The account is accurate at a general governance level, though “a small number” of reports redirecting the board is not a universal feature. Rating: 8

#### Beauty
This is a particularly well-shaped archetype. The template has a clear progression from partial information to aggregation, corruption resistance, urgency, and legitimacy–responsiveness tradeoff. The parallel prose is consistent without becoming entirely mechanical. It is slightly more elegant and focused than the Reference. Rating: 8

#### Intelligence
The abstraction is strong: it captures a common design problem across governance, biology, distributed computing, medicine, and management without reducing consensus to mere voting. The explicit tension between legitimacy and responsiveness is insightful. Rating: 9

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
The domains are very diverse and structurally distinct: political, biological, cryptographic, clinical, and corporate systems. The metanyms are generally domain-specific and far from synonymous, although “protocol,” “signal,” and “decision” are necessarily abstractly reused. This is clearly stronger than the Reference on cross-domain contrast. Rating: 9

#### Impressive length

### Archetypal context 3: Gradient-Driven Allocation

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Gas Diffusion): The account correctly describes diffusion down concentration gradients, barriers, and compressors doing work to maintain gradients. The wording that a molecule “tends to move” from high to low density is acceptable as a macroscopic shorthand. Rating: 9
- PC 2 (Labor Market): Workers may move toward better opportunities, barriers can preserve wage differences, and subsidies can induce counterflows. However, the template’s direction from “high wage opportunity” to “low wage opportunity” is backwards if interpreted literally, making the Form (a) materially incorrect. Rating: 4
- PC 3 (Water Basin): Water generally moves down hydraulic gradients, dams preserve differences, and pumps consume energy to move water uphill. The account is accurate at a high level, though “make the watershed more uniform” is imprecise. Rating: 8
- PC 4 (Information Ecosystem): Information can spread from highly attentive audiences to less attentive ones, while paywalls and promotion affect distribution. However, information does not spontaneously move according to an attention gradient in the same physical sense as gas or water, so the analogy is only conditionally factual. Rating: 6
- PC 5 (Urban Housing): The Form (a) direction is again reversed: households would generally move from low affordability toward high affordability, not from high affordability toward low affordability. Form (b) corrects the direction, but the required Form (a) remains factually wrong. Rating: 4

#### Beauty
The template is elegant as a physical transport abstraction, and the barrier/pump contrast is memorable. Its beauty is weakened by forcing social systems into a literal high-to-low concentration direction that does not fit labor and housing. Relative to the Reference, it is conceptually attractive but less consistently realized. Rating: 7

#### Intelligence
The underlying insight—that distributions reflect spontaneous equalization, barriers, and active counterflows—is strong. However, the submission does not adequately adapt the direction of the gradient to domains where agents seek higher rewards or affordability, revealing a failure to distinguish physical diffusion from motivated movement. Rating: 6

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
The domains range from gas physics and hydrology to labor, information, and housing, and the metanyms are mostly distinct. Yet the labor and housing mappings expose a semantic mismatch in “concentration” and “movement,” making the cross-domain transfer less convincing than the table suggests. Rating: 7

#### Impressive length
The template is reasonably developed, with nine slots and five sentences, but it is shorter and less elaborate than the Reference’s longer causal templates. Rating: 7

### Archetypal context 4: Feedback-Controlled Pursuit

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)

- PC 2 (Blood Glucose Regulation): The account is broadly correct, but it overstates the pancreas as the sole controller and glucose sensors as a single explicit sensor. Glucose regulation also involves the liver, nervous system, glucagon, and distributed sensing. Rating: 7
- PC 3 (Cruise Control): The description accurately captures speed sensing, throttle adjustment, error correction, and hunting under poor tuning. Rating: 9
- PC 4 (Monetary Policy): Central banks use indicators and policy rates to influence inflation, with risks from weak, excessive, or delayed responses. The causal lag and multidimensional nature of monetary policy are simplified, but the claims are sound. Rating: 8
- PC 5 (Project Management): Project managers monitor schedule variance and reallocate resources, while weak or excessive intervention can respectively permit delay or create churn. This is a reasonable and factually defensible abstraction. Rating: 8

#### Beauty
This is a clean, highly legible template with a classic control-loop structure. The parallel contexts are concise and the Form (b) rewrites improve readability without losing the mechanism. It is somewhat more repetitive than the Reference but has strong formal elegance. Rating: 8

#### Intelligence
The abstraction is technically grounded and transfers well across engineering, physiology, transportation, macroeconomics, and management. The explicit treatment of gain, delay, error, and oscillation demonstrates strong systems thinking. Rating: 9

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
The domains are meaningfully different, and the metanyms map well: heater, insulin secretion, throttle, policy rate, and resource allocation are distinct actuators rather than synonyms. The systems all share a recognizable control architecture, but that is the point of the archetype. Rating: 8

#### Impressive length
The template has ten slots and six sentences, giving it substantial but not exceptional length. It is comparable to the Reference’s medium-length templates. Rating: 7

### Archetypal context 5: Layered Translation

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Genetic Expression): The Form (b) account is broadly accurate about codons, anticodon pairing, amino-acid delivery, and translation fidelity. Form (a), however, incorrectly says the gene and ribosome use different nucleotide sequences and that tRNA converts codons into an “intermediate anticodon pairing”; mRNA is omitted from the main mapping. The justification partially acknowledges this, but the required sentence remains technically flawed. Rating: 5
- PC 2 (Computer Compilation): The account correctly describes source code, compilation, intermediate representation, machine code, and preservation of computational intent. The claim that source programs and processors use different “programming languages” is a simplification, but acceptable. Rating: 8
- PC 3 (Legal Interpretation): The account is a plausible abstraction of statutory interpretation, though a statute and court do not literally use different legal languages, and “intermediate legal construction” is theoretical rather than universal. Rating: 7
- PC 4 (International Diplomacy): Diplomats do interpret policy and formulate agreements that preserve intent while adapting language and political context. The account is necessarily schematic but factually defensible. Rating: 8
- PC 5 (Music Performance): The Form (a) sentence “a score cannot directly perform performance in an audience” is grammatically and conceptually defective, and the score/audience “use different notation” is inaccurate because audiences generally do not use notation. Form (b) repairs much of this, but the required Form (a) is materially wrong. Rating: 4

#### Beauty
The translation template has a compelling two-sided fidelity criterion: preserving meaning while adapting form. The parallel contexts are intellectually attractive, but the genetic and musical instantiations contain awkward or incorrect substitutions that damage the overall finish. Rating: 7

#### Intelligence
The distinction between surface form and underlying meaning is sophisticated and useful across compilation, law, diplomacy, and performance. However, the biological mapping mishandles the actual translation pathway, and the music mapping confuses notation with audience reception. The conceptual intelligence is high but execution is uneven. Rating: 8

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
The domains are impressively varied—molecular biology, computing, law, diplomacy, and music—and the metanyms are generally far from synonymous. The cross-domain span is among the strongest in the submission, though some mappings are forced. Rating: 9

#### Impressive length
The template is long and structurally rich, with ten slots and six sentences, including two distinct failure modes. It is comparable to or slightly better than the Reference’s typical template length. Rating: 8
