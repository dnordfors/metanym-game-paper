### Archetypal context 1: Constraint Propagation

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Electrical Circuit): Relative to the Reference, this is less factually reliable. Kirchhoff’s laws constrain voltages and currents, but “over-constrained nodes short-circuit” is not generally true, and current flow is not simply the information propagated by a voltage assignment. The final claim that the laws operate identically in physical, logical, and social forms is also false in-domain. Rating: 4
- PC 2 (Protein Folding): The rewrite captures local interactions influencing global conformation, but it overstates hydrogen bonds as the governing constraint network and incorrectly treats individual amino acids as entering a “misfolding state” because they cannot satisfy hydrogen-bond constraints. Protein folding does not generally oscillate indefinitely in the stated sense. Relative to the Reference, factual precision is weaker. Rating: 4
- PC 3 (Scheduling Problem): This is among the stronger instantiations. Precedence constraints, assignment propagation, feasible schedules, and infeasibility are appropriate, although “cycles” are more a solver behavior than an inherent outcome and CSP does not necessarily predict completion time. It is somewhat cleaner than several Reference Form (a) mappings. Rating: 7
- PC 4 (Ecosystem): Predation can constrain population dynamics and trophic cascades can propagate abundance changes, but “over-constrained species” and “solving community assembly” are not standard ecological claims. The assertion that trophic cascades operate identically across physical, logical, and social constraints is untenable. Rating: 4
- PC 5 (Social Network): Norms can constrain publicly expressed beliefs and contagion can produce consensus or polarization, but the mapping of belief adoption to “rumor,” and of over-constraint directly to polarization, is simplistic. The universal final sentence is again factually indefensible. Rating: 5

The underlying abstraction—local constraints narrowing neighboring possibilities—is genuinely useful and connects CSPs, circuits, folding, ecology, and social dynamics. However, it conflates distinct causal mechanisms and repeatedly claims identity across domains where only analogy holds. Relative to the Reference, the conceptual ambition is comparable but execution is less disciplined.

### Archetypal context 2: Boundary Dissolution

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Cell Membrane): The account correctly identifies selective permeability and oxidative membrane damage, but equating increased membrane fluidity with permeability is oversimplified, and lysis is not a deliberate response a cell “accepts.” The Form (b) is more defensible than Form (a), but overall it is below the Reference’s cellular-barrier treatment. Rating: 5
- PC 2 (Nation-State): Trade, border control, and cross-border threats are plausible analogues, but globalization does not inherently erode borders toward inevitable state dissolution, and “invasion penetration” is awkward and misleading. The deterministic collapse claim substantially weakens factual correctness. Rating: 4
- PC 3 (Immune System): Epithelial barriers and antigen sampling are real, but an epithelial barrier is not equivalent to the immune system as a whole, inflammation does not straightforwardly produce “immunosuppression,” and barrier failure does not make immune-system collapse inevitable. Relative to the Reference, this is materially less accurate. Rating: 4
- PC 4 (Coral Reef): Coral reefs do not function primarily as a boundary separating a symbiotic community from the open ocean, nor does reef structural permeability regulate heat penetration in the proposed way. Warming causes bleaching chiefly through coral–symbiont physiological stress, not penetration of a “bleaching agent.” Rating: 3
- PC 5 (Professional Guild): This is a plausible sociological metaphor, but credentials do not literally permit knowledge sharing while excluding fraud, and digitalization does not inherently cause credential inflation or inevitable professional collapse. It is coherent rhetorically but empirically overgeneralized. Rating: 5

Relative to the Reference, it is less nuanced.

### Archetypal context 3: Resonance and Damping

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Mechanical Oscillator): This is factually strong. Natural frequency, driven resonance, amplitude growth, frictional damping, and the trade-off between responsiveness and damping are all broadly correct. It is at least as accurate as the Reference’s stronger physical-science contexts. Rating: 8
- PC 2 (Electromagnetic Wave): Resonant cavities, standing modes, losses, field amplification, and dielectric breakdown are broadly correct. “Resistance” is an imperfect universal label for cavity loss, but Form (b) improves this with low-loss/high-loss terminology. Rating: 8
- PC 3 (Population Dynamics): Environmental forcing can resonate with intrinsic population cycles, and density dependence can damp fluctuations, but density dependence does not literally dissipate metabolic energy, “population design” is inappropriate, and beneficial resonance yielding stability is overstated. The submission itself admits its central term is nonstandard. Rating: 5
- PC 4 (Neural Circuit): Neural resonance, frequency tuning, inhibitory stabilization, and seizure risk from impaired inhibition are broadly sound. Still, inhibitory synapses do not literally dissipate neural activity or energy, and firing rate is not a direct analogue of oscillation amplitude in every case. Rating: 7
- PC 5 (Financial Market): Cycles, amplification, circuit breakers, and crash cascades are plausible, but circuit breakers do not dissipate capital; they halt trading. The claim that shocks arriving at a business-cycle frequency generate resonance is metaphorical rather than established finance. Rating: 5

This is one of the submission’s most aesthetically successful archetypes: the physical core gives the prose a strong cadence, and constructive versus destructive amplification is vivid. The weaker social and ecological mappings reduce consistency, but it is slightly more graceful than the Reference average.  
Rating: 8

#### Intelligence
The distinction between beneficial amplification and destructive runaway behavior, moderated by damping, is a strong systems insight. The archetype is especially intelligent in its physical and neural applications, though it becomes too literal in ecology and finance. Overall it exceeds the Reference modestly in conceptual clarity.

### Archetypal context 4: Nesting and Emergence

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Multicellular Organism): Hierarchical organization and neuroendocrine feedback are real, but consciousness cannot be confidently characterized as emerging from “synaptic connections between cells” broadly, since most cells are not synaptically connected, and consciousness constraining cellular metabolism is highly indirect. Relative to the Reference, this is somewhat speculative. Rating: 5
- PC 2 (Ecosystem): Ecosystems are hierarchical and trophic feedback can regulate reproduction, but biodiversity is not simply an emergent property arising from predation, nor are strongly coupled trophic-feedback ecosystems necessarily less productive. The claims are plausible at a high level but too categorical. Rating: 5
- PC 3 (Language): Words and phonemes form a hierarchy, grammar is not present in individual words, and grammatical conventions constrain interpretation. However, grammar cannot be said to arise from syntax alone, since syntax is itself part of grammar, making the explanation circular. Rating: 6
- PC 4 (Immune System): Immune tolerance is system-level and regulatory T cells contribute importantly to it. Still, genes do not simply “compose lymphocytes” in the relevant hierarchical sense, a regulatory T cell is not a feedback loop, and tolerance is not reducible to interactions among lymphocytes. Rating: 5
- PC 5 (Economic System): Firms, workers, competition, and price feedback are reasonable components of an economic-system account. Yet market efficiency is not reliably an emergent property of competition alone, and tight price feedback does not straightforwardly make economies less entrepreneurial. Rating: 6

#### Beauty
The recursive framing is elegant and the scale notation gives the template a distinctive formal character. Some substitutions are grammatically awkward—especially “strong regulatory T cell”—but the overall presentation is more memorable than the Reference average.  
Rating: 7

#### Intelligence
This is the submission’s clearest attempt at genuine recursion, and it identifies hierarchy, emergence, and downward feedback as a unified systems structure. That is a valuable and ambitious insight. Its weakness is treating contested emergent properties as settled and using overly simple component relations, but it remains stronger than the Reference on recursive ambition.  
Rating: 8

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
Organisms, ecosystems, language, immunity, and economies are highly distinct domains. The metanyms—cells, organisms, words, lymphocytes, firms—are far from synonymous and instantiate the hierarchy in varied ways.  
Rating: 8

#### Impressive length
The template is substantial but shorter than the Reference’s most elaborate archetypes. It has a clear hierarchy, emergence claim, feedback mechanism, robustness/flexibility trade-off, and field-level conclusion.  
Rating: 7

### Archetypal context 5: Threshold and Tipping

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Climate System): Climate tipping elements and hysteresis are legitimate concepts, but “runaway warming” from CO2 crossing a single tipping point is misleading, negative feedbacks do not simply fail wholesale, and carbon sequestration can contribute to climate recovery even where some tipping processes are irreversible. Relative to the Reference, accuracy is weaker. Rating: 5
- PC 2 (Ecosystem Collapse): Critical transitions, resilience, hysteresis, and degradation thresholds are valid ecological concepts. However, the claim that restoration cannot recover prior states is too absolute, and “critical transition” is not the instability condition itself. Rating: 6
- PC 3 (Neural Firing): Threshold firing is correctly described in broad terms, but action potentials are not generally hysteretic in the template’s stated way, and requiring input current to fall below threshold before quiescence can be restored is inaccurate. The Form (b) confuses refractory-period threshold elevation with hysteresis. Rating: 5
- PC 4 (Social Movement): Threshold models of collective action exist, but revolution is not generally irreversible, grievance reduction can matter, and the deterministic transition framing oversimplifies political processes. It is a plausible analogy rather than a factually robust account. Rating: 5
- PC 5 (Organizational Change): Organizations can face nonlinear collapse and inertia, but the universal claims of irreversibility, hysteresis, and rebuilding-from-scratch are unsupported. This is rhetorically coherent but empirically overgeneralized. Rating: 5

#### Beauty
The template has a compelling dramatic arc—stability, mounting pressure, threshold crossing, transition, and hysteresis—and it reads smoothly across domains. Repetition of “often irreversible” makes it somewhat formulaic, but it is aesthetically stronger than the Reference average.  
Rating: 8

#### Intelligence
Critical transitions and hysteresis are important systems concepts, and the archetype captures their broad explanatory appeal. Yet it applies bistability and irreversibility too indiscriminately, especially to neurons, climate, politics, and organizations. Relative to the Reference, it is conceptually strong but insufficiently discriminating.  
Rating: 7

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
Climate, ecology, neurophysiology, political change, and organizational theory are substantially different domains, with clearly non-synonymous metanyms. This matches or modestly exceeds the Reference’s domain diversity.  
Rating: 8

#### Impressive length
The template is long and structurally rich, including parameter variation, stabilization, threshold crossing, irreversibility, instability, hysteresis, prediction, and intervention. It is comparable to the Reference’s strongest templates in length.  
Rating: 8
