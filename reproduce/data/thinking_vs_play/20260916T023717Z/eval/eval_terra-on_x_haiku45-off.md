### Archetypal context 1: Constraint Propagation

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Electrical Circuit): Relative to the Reference, this instantiation is less factually reliable. Kirchhoff’s laws do constrain circuit variables, but an “over-constrained node” does not thereby enter a short-circuit state, and assigning a voltage does not literally make current flow propagate as constraint information. The final claim that these physical laws operate identically in logical or social systems is also false. Rating: 5
- PC 2 (Protein Folding): Relative to the Reference, the core analogy is plausible but overly reductive. Hydrogen bonding is only one of several major determinants of folding, individual amino acids do not themselves “enter a misfolding state,” and protein folding does not ordinarily oscillate indefinitely in the stated sense. The assertion that folding operates identically for physical, logical, and social constraints is especially inapt. Rating: 5
- PC 3 (Scheduling Problem): This is among the stronger instantiations relative to the Reference. Precedence constraints, narrowing temporal assignments, propagation, and infeasibility are all recognizable scheduling/CSP concepts. “Oscillation indefinitely” is less standard for constraint propagation than nonconvergence or repeated search, and predicting completion time is not inherent to every CSP, but the factual core is sound. Rating: 6
- PC 4 (Ecosystem): Relative to the Reference, this application forces the constraint-satisfaction vocabulary too literally onto ecology. Predator-prey interactions affect viable abundances, but species do not become “over-constrained” and thereby enter extinction states, and trophic cascades are not simply a generic solver that converges, diverges, or oscillates. Rating: 5
- PC 5 (Social Network): The account captures aspects of social influence and polarization, but it conflates norms, private beliefs, rumors, and contagion. Polarization is not generally the result of agents being over-constrained, and the final cross-domain claim remains inaccurate. It is somewhat less factually secure than the Reference’s stronger social mappings. Rating: 5

#### Beauty
The template has crisp logical progression and an appealing movement from local constraint to global configuration. However, its prose is more technical and schematic than evocative, while repeated wording such as “propagates,” “constraints,” and “failure” makes the parallel forms feel mechanically generated. Relative to the Reference’s generally polished archetypal narratives, it is modestly less elegant.  
Rating: 6

#### Intelligence
The abstraction of local commitments narrowing neighboring possibilities is genuinely intelligent and maps especially well to CSPs, circuits, and some folding models. Yet the submission overstates identity across physical, logical, and social systems, and several mappings treat rich causal systems as if they were formal constraint solvers. Relative to the Reference, the insight is good but less carefully discriminated.  
Rating: 6

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
The five domains—electrical circuits, molecular biology, scheduling, ecology, and social networks—are substantially separated, and terms such as voltage, conformation, time slot, population size, and belief are appropriately non-synonymous metanyms. This is more domainally ambitious than much of the Reference.  
Rating: 8

#### Impressive length
The template is a substantial, coherent paragraph with enough slots to establish an actual system dynamic rather than a brief analogy. It is comparable in length and complexity to the Reference templates, though not especially longer or more elaborate.

### Archetypal context 2: Boundary Dissolution

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Cell Membrane): Relative to the Reference, this is somewhat weaker factually. Membranes permit selective molecular transport, but “nutrient uptake” is usually transporter-mediated rather than simple diffusion, and oxidative stress does not uniformly increase membrane fluidity in the stated way. Cells also do not meaningfully choose to “accept lysis,” though the general barrier-damage account is sound. Rating: 5
- PC 2 (Nation-State): The analogy is rhetorically clear but factually overgeneralized. Globalization does not inherently erode national borders toward state dissolution, and increased trade or border porosity does not imply invasion penetration or inevitable national collapse. This is materially less grounded than the Reference’s better-developed institutional contexts. Rating: 4
- PC 3 (Immune System): Epithelial barriers do regulate antigen sampling and pathogen exclusion, and inflammation can impair barrier integrity. However, the immune system is not itself straightforwardly bounded by an epithelial barrier, immunosuppression is not the direct analogue of barrier dissolution, and critical epithelial damage does not inevitably produce collapse of the immune system. Rating: 4
- PC 4 (Coral Reef): This is the weakest factual mapping. Reef structure does not principally separate a symbiotic community from the open ocean, warming is not a penetrating “bleaching agent” excluded by reef structure, and physical permeability of reef structure is not the mechanism controlling coral bleaching. Relative to the Reference, the analogy is substantially less accurate. Rating: 3
- PC 5 (Professional Guild): Credentialing can regulate entry and digital systems can facilitate fraud or credential inflation, but credentials do not literally separate professional membership from a public exterior or control knowledge-sharing flow in the proposed way. The predicted inevitability of professional collapse is also overstated. Rating: 4

The erosion-versus-exchange tension is intuitively vivid and gives the template a strong narrative arc. Nevertheless, it closely resembles the Reference’s containment-breach archetype while replacing its concrete cascade mechanism with broad, often melodramatic claims of irreversible collapse. Relative to the Reference, it is less original and less precise in its imagery.  
Rating: 5

#### Intelligence
The central tradeoff between permeability and protection is an important general systems insight. Yet the submission mistakes several superficially similar barriers for instances of the same material process, particularly in reefs and professional credentialing. Relative to the Reference’s containment model, it displays a worthwhile abstraction but weaker causal discipline.  
Rating: 5

#### Domains far apart / metanyms not synonymous
The selection spans cells, states, immune barriers, reef ecology, and professional institutions. Cell membranes and epithelial barriers are relatively close biological domains, but the other domains are far apart and the metanyms remain distinct rather than synonymous. This is roughly comparable to the Reference.  
Rating: 7

#### Impressive length
The template is well-developed, containing controlled exchange, erosion, adaptive responses, thresholds, and a disciplinary perspective. Its length is comparable to the Reference’s containment template.  
Rating: 7

Rating: 8
- PC 2 (Electromagnetic Wave): The Form (b) account of low-loss cavities is broadly accurate, but Form (a) reverses an important relation by describing low resistance as making cavities resonance-vulnerable; low resistance in cavity walls corresponds to low loss and high quality factor, whereas high resistance increases dissipation. The mixed accuracy leaves it slightly below the Reference. Rating: 6

It remains reasonably sound overall. Rating: 6

Circuit breakers halt trading rather than dissipate capital, business cycles do not have a stable natural frequency in the mechanical sense, and the proposed constructive “market resonance” account is speculative. Rating: 4

Relative to the Reference, it is about equally polished, despite weaker peripheral applications.

### Archetypal context 4: Nesting and Emergence

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Multicellular Organism): The hierarchical organization and importance of neural interaction and neuroendocrine feedback are broadly correct. However, consciousness is not an established simple global property derivable from cells in the asserted way, and the claim that strong neuroendocrine loops make organisms less flexible is too broad. Relative to the Reference, this is moderately sound but philosophically and scientifically overstated. Rating: 6
- PC 2 (Ecosystem): Organisms form ecosystems, biodiversity is a system-level property, and trophic feedback can stabilize ecological dynamics. But biodiversity does not arise from predation alone, and strong trophic feedback does not generally make ecosystems less productive. The form is conceptually plausible but simplified. Rating: 6
- PC 3 (Language): Phonemes, words, syntax, grammar, semantic constraints, and historical convention do support a coherent emergence account. The claim that grammar cannot be predicted from word meaning alone is defensible, though grammar does not simply emerge synchronically from syntax “between words,” and rigidity-versus-innovation is generalized. This is near the Reference’s factual standard. Rating: 6
- PC 4 (Immune System): Immune tolerance is indeed a systemic outcome of interactions among immune cell types, including regulatory T cells. Yet “genes compose lymphocytes” is a rough category simplification, and a feedback loop does not “create a regulatory T cell”; it uses regulatory T cells and signals. Relative to the Reference, it is broadly credible but imprecise. Rating: 6
- PC 5 (Economic System): Competition and price signals can produce system-level allocation patterns, but market efficiency is neither guaranteed nor a straightforward emergent property absent institutional assumptions. The claim that tight price feedback makes economies less entrepreneurial is similarly overgeneralized. Rating: 6

### Archetypal context 5: Threshold and Tipping

#### Factually correct (per parallel context)
- PC 1 (Climate System): Climate tipping points, feedbacks, critical slowing, and hysteresis are legitimate concepts. However, the account overstates the certainty of runaway warming, treats uncertain cloud feedback as straightforwardly negative, and incorrectly implies carbon sequestration could not in principle help restore prior conditions. Relative to the Reference, it is somewhat less factually careful. Rating: 5
- PC 2 (Ecosystem Collapse): Resilience, critical transitions, hysteresis, and ecological regime shifts are well-established concepts. Still, the claims that a barren transition is often irreversible and that restoration cannot recover prior conditions are overbroad. It is a plausible but simplified mapping, below the Reference’s factual standard. Rating: 5
- PC 3 (Neural Firing): The threshold mechanism and action potential dynamics are real, but the archetype misuses irreversibility and hysteresis. Resting potential does not “fail,” firing is transient rather than a new stable behavior, and post-spike refractoriness raises the threshold rather than requiring input current to fall below threshold for firing. This is substantially less accurate than the Reference. Rating: 4
- PC 4 (Social Movement): Threshold models of collective action and revolutionary cascades are meaningful, and social control can stabilize order. Nevertheless, revolution is not generally irreversible, and the universal causal language about collapse and restoration oversimplifies political history. Rating: 5
- PC 5 (Organizational Change): Organizations can exhibit inertia, nonlinear stress responses, and collapse cascades. But organizational collapse is not necessarily irreversible or unrecoverable except through rebuilding from scratch, so the template imposes a stronger bistability claim than the domain supports. Rating: 5

The template presents a familiar but powerful dramatic structure: stability, accumulating pressure, threshold crossing, irreversible transformation, and hysteresis. Its prose is direct and clear, and its conceptual arc is as aesthetically effective as the Reference’s archetypes, though repeated use of “collapse” reduces subtlety.
