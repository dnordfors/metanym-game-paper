- PC 1 (Plant): The statements about plants undergoing growth through nutrient accumulation, adapting to environmental changes, and the risk of wilting are botanically accurate. Plants do reallocate nutrients (e.g., from older to newer leaves) in response to stress, and botany does study these processes. The claim about homeostasis is appropriate for plant physiology. Rating: 9

- PC 2 (Economy): The economic parallel is largely accurate—economies do grow through capital accumulation, and market changes necessitate capital reallocation. However, the framing that economies "must adapt" anthropomorphizes economic systems somewhat, and the relationship between capital reallocation and equilibrium is more nuanced than presented. Recessions have multiple causes beyond failure to adapt. Rating: 7

- PC 3 (Software System): This parallel contains inaccuracies. Software systems don't primarily "develop" through accumulation of computational power—development refers to code creation, not resource accumulation. The conflation of development (building software) with scaling (handling demand) is conceptually confused. Computational power is not accumulated but allocated. Rating: 4

- PC 4 (Human Body): The human body parallel is reasonably accurate. Growth does require energy, and the body reallocates energy during health changes. However, "growth" in adults is limited, and the relationship between energy reallocation and illness prevention is oversimplified. Medicine does study metabolic allocation. Rating: 7

- PC 5 (Ecosystem): Ecosystems do expand through resource utilization, and climate change requires adaptation. The concept of ecosystem "balance" is somewhat outdated in modern ecology (dynamic equilibrium is preferred), but the general framework is acceptable. Ecology does study resource allocation. Rating: 8

The parallel between homeostasis across biological and economic systems has some elegance, but the overall construction feels workmanlike rather than beautiful. The Form (b) rewrites improve readability but don't elevate the prose significantly.

The template doesn't reveal surprising structural similarities or generate non-obvious predictions.

### Structural diversity across the submitted set

The five archetypal contexts share significant structural similarities. All five follow a pattern of: (1) entity/agent performs/undergoes activity, (2) conditions change requiring adaptation, (3) failure occurs without adaptation, (4) a discipline studies this. This four-part structure (action, change, failure, study) is repeated across all templates with minor variations. The templates differ in their specific focus (growth, conflict, innovation, exploration, structure-function), but the underlying logical structure is remarkably uniform. None of the templates include feedback loops, recursive structures, branching conditions, or multi-agent interactions that would demonstrate structural diversity. The submission would benefit from templates with fundamentally different architectures—perhaps one focused on cycles, one on hierarchies, one on networks, etc. The consistent inclusion of a "STUDY" slot across all templates further homogenizes the structures.
