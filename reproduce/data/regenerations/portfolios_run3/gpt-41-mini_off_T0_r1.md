### River System

**Form (a):**  
A spring provides water that flows through a river to reach a lake. Along the way, tributaries modify or convert the water, affecting its quality and quantity. The efficiency of the river and the capacity of the lake determine the overall throughput. Disruptions in the spring, river, or tributaries cause drought, reducing the availability of water at the lake. Understanding the dynamics of water flow and transformation is essential for optimizing system performance and preventing drought.

**Form (b):**  
A spring feeds water into a river system that ultimately empties into a lake. Tributaries join the river, altering the water’s volume and quality. The river’s flow efficiency and the lake’s capacity govern how much water arrives downstream. Interruptions such as droughts in any part of this system reduce water availability at the lake. Studying these flow dynamics helps manage water resources and mitigate drought impacts.

### Supply Chain

**Form (a):**  
A factory provides goods that flow through transportation to reach a retailer. Along the way, warehouses modify or convert the goods, affecting their quality and quantity. The efficiency of transportation and the capacity of the retailer determine the overall throughput. Disruptions in the factory, transportation, or warehouses cause shortage, reducing the availability of goods at the retailer. Understanding the dynamics of goods flow and transformation is essential for optimizing system performance and preventing shortage.

**Form (b):**  
Factories produce goods that move through transportation networks to retailers. Warehouses along the route handle, store, and sometimes repackage goods, influencing their condition and volume. The speed of transportation and retailer capacity dictate how much product reaches consumers. Disruptions like transportation delays or warehouse failures cause shortages at retail outlets. Supply chain management focuses on these flows to ensure product availability and avoid shortages.

### Neural Network

**Form (a):**  
A sensory neuron provides a signal that flows through an axon to reach a motor neuron. Along the way, interneurons modify or convert the signal, affecting its quality and quantity. The efficiency of the axon and the capacity of the motor neuron determine the overall throughput. Disruptions in the sensory neuron, axon, or interneurons cause signal loss, reducing the availability of signal at the motor neuron. Understanding the dynamics of signal flow and transformation is essential for optimizing system performance and preventing signal loss.

**Form (b):**  
Sensory neurons transmit signals along axons to motor neurons, with interneurons modulating these signals en route. The integrity of axonal transmission and motor neuron responsiveness determine how effectively signals propagate. Damage or dysfunction in any component can cause signal loss, impairing motor responses. Neuroscience studies these pathways to understand and mitigate neural communication failures.

Market efficiency and consumer demand govern the volume of goods exchanged. Disruptions like market crashes or intermediary failures reduce commodity availability. Economic analysis of these flows informs policies to stabilize markets and protect consumers.

**Form (b):**  
Primary producers like plants capture energy that moves through food webs to apex predators. Decomposers recycle nutrients and energy, influencing ecosystem productivity. The structure of the food web and predator populations regulate energy transfer efficiency. Disruptions such as species loss or environmental stress can cause ecosystem collapse, reducing energy availability at the top. Ecology studies these flows to maintain ecosystem health and resilience.

# Summary

These five archetypal contexts represent very different system structures:

1. **Resource Flow and Transformation** — linear flow with transformation and sinks.
2. **Competitive Exclusion and Niche Partitioning** — multi-agent competition and coexistence.
3. **Feedback Regulation and Control** — closed-loop control with feedback.
4. **Information Encoding and Decoding** — communication with encoding, transmission, and decoding.
5. **Growth and Saturation Dynamics** — growth processes limited by constraints.
6. **Hierarchical Delegation and Authority** — layered command and control with delegation.

(Though six are listed here, only five are required; the last one is included for completeness and can be omitted if needed.)

Each archetype spans five very different domains with non-synonymous metanyms, demonstrating broad applicability and deep abstraction.
