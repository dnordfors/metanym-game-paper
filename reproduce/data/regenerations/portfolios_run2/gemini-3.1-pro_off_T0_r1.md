### Host-Pathogen Coevolution

**Form (a)**:
"A host must constantly adapt its immune response to counter the evolving virulence factors of a pathogen. As the host develops a new immune response, the pathogen experiences selective pressure to overcome it, leading to a continuous cycle of mutation. This coevolutionary arms race drives rapid genetic variation in both host and pathogen, often resulting in highly specialized and complex resistance genes. However, this constant mutation comes at a metabolic cost, as resources are diverted from other essential reproduction. If the host fails to keep pace, it risks infection, while the pathogen risks infection if it cannot overcome the immune response. Evolutionary biology studies the dynamics of this coevolutionary arms race to understand the resulting genetic diversity and immune system complexity."

**Form (b)**:
"Hosts and pathogens are locked in a perpetual struggle, where the host's immune defenses must continuously evolve to thwart the pathogen's ever-changing virulence strategies. Each new host defense creates intense selective pressure on the pathogen to adapt, fueling a relentless cycle of mutation. This coevolutionary arms race accelerates genetic variation, producing highly specialized traits like novel resistance genes. Yet, this constant adaptation exacts a metabolic toll, diverting energy from vital functions like reproduction. A host that falls behind faces infection, while a pathogen unable to breach defenses faces eradication. Evolutionary biologists analyze these dynamics to explain the resulting genetic diversity and the intricate complexity of immune systems."

### Social Media

**Form (a)**:
"The utility of a social network to a subscriber increases as more subscribers join the user base. This positive network effect creates a strong incentive for sign-ups, as early subscribers attract more subscribers, further increasing the utility. However, this dynamic also creates a significant switching cost for competing social networks, as they struggle to attract subscribers without an established user base. The dominant social network often achieves a market dominance position, allowing it to dictate privacy policies and extract advertising revenue. If the social network fails to maintain its utility or introduces unfavorable privacy policies, it risks user defection, where subscribers migrate to a competing social network. Network economics studies the dynamics of these network externalities to understand market concentration and platform competition."

**Form (b)**:
"The utility of a social network grows exponentially as more subscribers join its user base. This positive network effect drives rapid adoption; early adopters attract their peers, which in turn increases the platform's overall value. However, this same dynamic creates formidable switching costs for new, competing social networks, which struggle to gain traction without an existing user base. Consequently, the dominant platform often achieves market dominance, empowering it to unilaterally dictate privacy policies and maximize advertising revenue. Yet, if the platform degrades its user experience or imposes overly restrictive policies, it risks mass user defection to a rival service. Network economists analyze these network externalities to understand the resulting market concentration and the nature of platform competition."
