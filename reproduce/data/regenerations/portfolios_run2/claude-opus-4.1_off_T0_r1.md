**Form (a)**: "A small injury enters the coagulation system and activates tissue factor, which then activates multiple downstream clotting factors. Anticoagulant mechanisms exist at each level to prevent runaway coagulation cascades and ensure the clot formation is proportional to the bleeding. When anticoagulants fail, thrombosis results from excessive thrombin burst. The coagulation cascade architecture trades platelet efficiency for speed and sensitivity, allowing rapid hemostasis when bleeding is detected."

**Form (b)**: "When tissue injury occurs, exposed tissue factor triggers the coagulation cascade — each activated clotting factor enzymatically activates many more in an exponential amplification called the thrombin burst. The cascade design sacrifices platelet efficiency for rapid, sensitive hemostatic response to bleeding threats."
